📚 Geography Notes

Explore introductory notes. These are learning summaries, not complete lecture notes.

Physical Geography

Study landforms, weather, climate, soils and natural processes.

Read summary

Physical geography examines Earth's natural systems. Major topics include geomorphology, climatology, hydrology, biogeography and soil geography.

Human Geography

People, settlements, population and human activities.

Read summary

Human geography explores population distribution, migration, urbanisation, culture and how people interact with their environments.

Cartography

The science and art of making maps.

Read summary

Important map elements include title, scale, legend, north arrow, symbols and source. Map projections represent Earth's curved surface on a flat map.

Hydrology

Water movement and distribution on Earth.

Read summary

The hydrological cycle includes evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, surface runoff and groundwater flow.

Remote Sensing & GIS

Collecting and analysing geographical data.

Read summary

Remote sensing collects information from a distance, often using satellites. GIS stores, manages, analyses and displays geographically referenced data.

Population Geography

Population size, distribution and change.

Read summary

Population change is influenced by fertility, mortality and migration. Population density is population divided by land area.

Economic Geography

Location of industries and economic activities.

Read summary

Economic geography examines the location of agriculture, industries, transport, trade and services, including factors influencing their distribution.

Environmental Geography

Relationships between people and the environment.

Read summary

Key themes include conservation, pollution, biodiversity, climate change, natural resources and sustainable development.

📝 Revision Corner

Test your understanding with these introductory questions.

Question 1

What is Geography?

Show answer

Geography is the study of Earth's places, environments, spatial patterns and relationships between people and their surroundings.

Question 2

Name four essential elements of a map.

Show answer

Examples include a title, scale, legend and north arrow.

Question 3

What is GIS?

Show answer

GIS stands for Geographic Information System. It is used to collect, manage, analyse and display spatial data.

Question 4

Calculate population density for 20,000 people living in 100 km².

Show answer

Population density = population ÷ area = 20,000 ÷ 100 = 200 people per km².
